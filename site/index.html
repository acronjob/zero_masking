01 · The question

How little of a pretrained model has to change?

Post-training methods differ in what kind of edit they make to a weight matrix. Deletion Tuning goes furthest: it adds nothing and only removes.

Full fine-tuning

Every value moves a little. Checkpoint = a full copy of the model.

LoRA

Adds a low-rank update BA. Rank capped at r; touches every entry.

Random sparse FT

Rewrites a few values at randomly pre-chosen positions.

Deletion Tuning

Learns which few weights to zero. No new values are ever written.

Animated: each tile flips between the pretrained matrix and the edited matrix. Cell color is the weight value (blue negative, orange positive).

02 · Where the mask goes

Only the gate projection of each MLP

Modern LLMs use SwiGLU MLPs. The gate projection decides, per channel, how much of the up_proj signal gets through to down_proj. It acts as a multiplicative switchboard, which makes it a natural place to cut wires.

  • Frozen: attention, embeddings, up_proj, down_proj, and the gate weight values themselves.
  • Trainable: one logit Gij per gate weight.
  • In our model (Qwen3.5-0.8B: 24 blocks, 18 Gated-DeltaNet + 6 full-attention): 24 × (3584 × 1024) = 88.1 M maskable weights, which is 11.7% of the 752 M text parameters.
03 · The parameterization

One logit per weight, squeezed through a very steep sigmoid

W̃ = W ⊙ σ(α(t) · G)
  • Training uses the soft mask above, so gradients reach G.
  • Rollouts and eval use the hard mask W · 1[G > 0], the thing you actually ship.
  • α anneals 10³ → 10⁴ over training, so soft and hard masks converge.
  • Regularizer −λ‖W̃‖² (λ = 10⁻⁵) rewards keeping weights, so a deletion has to pay for itself in reward.
Drag α. With logits initialized at ±0.03, the sigmoid is already saturated at α = 1000. In fp32, σ′(z) underflows to exactly 0 for |z| ≳ 16.6, so only logits inside a thin band around 0 receive any gradient. AdamW rescales those tiny gradients into full-size steps. As α grows, the band narrows and the mask freezes in place.

04 · Initialization

Start from a pruning prior: Wanda

Each weight gets an importance score from one calibration pass:

sij = |Wij| · ‖Xj‖2

The bottom η = 1/2048 of each layer starts deleted (logit ≈ N(−0.03, 0.01²)), and everything else starts kept (≈ N(+0.03, 0.01²)). Pruning by Wanda alone barely moves accuracy (within 2.4 pts of base in the paper), so the gains come from learning the mask, not from the initialization.

What we found in the init masks

η/layer is only 1,792 weights, yet ~6,740 start deleted. The other ~4,950 come from the noise tail: a kept logit N(+0.03, 0.01²) falls below 0 with p = Φ(−3) ≈ 0.135%. Together they reproduce the paper's "≈0.18%" figure.

05 · Reinforcement learning

GRPO: sample with the hard mask, learn through the soft one

One prompt group (n = 4 rollouts)

Reward is 1 if the number after #### matches the GSM8K answer. Advantage = (r − mean) / std within the group.
  • Fully on-policy: one gradient step per batch, so the PPO ratio ≡ 1 and the clipped objective reduces to −A · log π. No KL term.
  • Per step: 32 prompts × 4 rollouts, ≤ 768 new tokens, about 17 s on one RTX PRO 6000.
  • Only 88 M logits get gradients. The optimizer is AdamW with lr 3·10⁻⁴, constant.
06 · What the paper found

Matches full fine-tuning at scale, with an adapter ~4,000× smaller

GRPO results (best-checkpoint validation accuracy). Llama models are trained and evaluated on GSM8K. Qwen2.5 models are trained on the SimpleRL-Zoo math data and scored as the mean of six math benchmarks. The gap is largest on the smallest models, and Deletion Tuning edges out FFT on the largest ones.

07 · Why it's interesting

Tiny, barely forgets, and updates like FFT

Adapter size · Llama 8B (log scale)

The mask compresses to ~0.03 bits per target weight (zstd), smaller even than LoRA rank 1.

Forgetting · range across Llama 1B/3B/8B

Prior-task suite: HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA, WinoGrande, IFEval, HumanEval. MTH-99.95% trains a similar 0.05% of parameters, so a small edit count alone doesn't explain the retention.

Update geometry · stable rank of ΔW

LoRA is pinned at rank 1–2 and random-sparse updates are noise-like (410–650). Deletion Tuning lands in between, and its spectrum drift and principal-weight overlap track FFT most closely.

11 · Honest caveats

What this does not show yet

  • One main run (seed 0). +4.8 pts is about 2 standard errors at n = 500, and "best" is the max over 8 evals, which biases it upward. Seeds 1–3 are running now.
  • The start point already shifts. Different Wanda/noise masks alone give step-0 accuracies from 54.4% to 55.8%.
  • No baselines yet. Without FFT, LoRA, or random-sparse under the same budget, we can't say whether deletion is competitive here, only that it learns.
  • No retention suite. The paper's headline (no forgetting) is untested on this model.
  • Shorter rollouts (768 vs 4096 tokens) and a strong instruct base (54% at step 0, vs Llama 1B at 1.2%) leave less headroom.
12 · Next steps

Where to take it

Baselines

FFT (lr 10⁻⁶), LoRA r=1/4, and MTH-99.95% with identical rollouts and budget.

Forgetting

Forward KL on GSM8K, plus a retention suite (MMLU, HellaSwag, IFEval…).

Init ablation

Remove the noise tail (σ = 0) to separate Wanda deletions from random ones.

α / band

Since the mask freezes by ~step 75, try a slower α ramp or smaller init |G| to keep more logits trainable.

Mask arithmetic

Union or intersect masks from different tasks or seeds (the paper lists merging as future work).

Full GSM8K

Score all 1,319 test problems and report the final checkpoint alongside the best one.
